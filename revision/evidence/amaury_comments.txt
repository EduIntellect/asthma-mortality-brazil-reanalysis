Dear Frederico,

Thank you for sending the revised manuscript. I have read it carefully and
consider it a valuable contribution to interpreting asthma mortality trends
in Brazil. The distinction between crude, age-standardised and age-specific
mortality is clearly presented, and the non-causal interpretation of the
decomposition is appropriate.

My assessment is favourable, subject to several clarifications before
submission:

   1.

   *Epidemiological interpretation.* I agree that the crude increase was
   not accompanied by consistent evidence of a comparable age-standardised
   increase. However, the national standardised analysis concerns people aged
   seven years and older, which should remain explicit throughout.
   Non-significance should not be interpreted as proof of stability,
   especially because one specification using the 2024 population projection
   identified a positive trend. The 4% decline between the endpoints should
   also remain distinct from the trend estimated across all eight years.
   2.

   *Age standardisation.* The fixed Brazil 2014 standard is appropriate for
   temporal comparisons, and the WHO sensitivity analysis strengthens the
   findings. Please include the formula and clarify that the weights sum to
   one within each analysed age range. The treatment of ages ≥90 and the
   allocation of three fifths of the WHO 5–9-year weight to ages 7–9 should
   also be documented, with the latter identified as an approximation.
   3.

   *Age definitions and denominators.* I suggest adding the aligned crude
   series for completed ages ≥7 to Table 1. This is the series used alongside
   standardisation and decomposition, increasing from 1.123 to 1.258 per
   100,000. It differs slightly from the continuous-age >6 series currently
   shown. It would also be useful to verify completed age using calendar
   dates, as dividing elapsed days by 365.25 can misclassify records near age
   boundaries.
   4.

   *Kitagawa decomposition.* The interpretation is sound. Contributions
   above 100% indicate that the positive age-composition component was partly
   offset by declining age-specific rates. Please add the original
   methodological reference and equations, and explain minor rounding
   differences in Table S6. Repeating the decomposition with the 2024
   population projection would provide a useful sensitivity analysis.
   5.

   *Mortality at ages 5–34.* This is an important finding, particularly
   because the increase persists after standardisation within the age group. I
   suggest highlighting that result in the abstract and reporting the absolute
   endpoint increase, approximately 0.037 deaths per 100,000, alongside the
   relative change. An assessment of the influence of 2020 and a pre-pandemic
   sensitivity analysis would help establish how dependent the finding is on
   the pandemic years. I agree with avoiding a firm claim of a historical
   trend reversal.
   6.

   *Record selection and statistical models.* Please report COVID-related
   exclusions and duplicate removals separately. Deduplication based on dates
   and causes should be checked because distinct individuals may share those
   characteristics. The COVID filter should also be audited for U07.1, which
   is not explicitly mentioned in the manuscript, and a sensitivity analysis
   retaining COVID mentions would be informative. For the age-adjusted count
   models, please specify the nine age bands, model formula and
   confidence-interval methods, and clarify the differences between
   quasi-Poisson and negative binomial results.

These comments concern the coherence and reporting of the manuscript;
independent verification of the numerical results would require checking
the underlying data and code.

The funding and conflict-of-interest statements are appropriately worded,
provided they accurately reflect both authors’ circumstances. We should
confirm these declarations before submission. I also agree that the ethics
requirements should be checked with the journal and the relevant
institutional guidance, without claiming a formally granted exemption
unless this is documented.

Regarding publication without author fees, my first suggestion is
Respiratory Research & Clinical Practice, formerly Jornal Brasileiro de
Pneumologia. It offers open-access publication without submission,
processing or publication charges and is a particularly relevant venue for
this reanalysis.

Respiratory Medicine and Public Health are additional options, both
offering publication without publication fees under their subscription
model. Optional paid open access would not be necessary for that route. My
preference would be the former for a respiratory medicine emphasis and the
latter for a population surveillance emphasis.

After these clarifications and verification of the analytical outputs, I
believe we will have a stronger manuscript ready for journal selection.

Best regards,
Amaury
